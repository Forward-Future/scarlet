# Fit a target length

Bring the edit to the length the editor asked for by taking out what the video needs least, and keep everything it needs to make its point. Read the edit as it plays now, from start to finish, before choosing anything.

## What goes first

Take out whole passages before tightening lines. A video that loses a tangent still feels like itself; one with every breath squeezed out feels rushed. In rough order of what the video misses least:

- Asides and tangents that don't come back.
- A second example, story or demo that makes the same point as one already kept. Keep the clearer one.
- Recaps of what the viewer has just seen, and setup they don't need to follow what comes next.
- Long build-ups to a point that lands as well when it arrives sooner.
- Only then, pauses and filler a dialogue pass would cut, if they are still there.

Keep the opening's promise and whatever pays it off, anything the end refers back to, and anything the editor's brief names. When a passage is the only place something the video needs is said, it stays.

## How each cut lands

Each passage that goes is one edit, cut between sentences or thoughts. The line after it has to make sense without what went: no “as I said” pointing at removed words, no “the second thing” when the first is gone, no answer whose question was cut. When a join only works by dropping a few more words, drop them. Check every join as it will be heard.

## Hitting the number

Stop when the edit reaches the target. “Under ten minutes” means under; “about eight” means within a few seconds of it. Don't cut past the target because more could go.

When the target can only be reached by cutting something the video needs, cut what clearly doesn't belong and offer the rest as suggestions, each saying in one sentence what the viewer would lose. The editor decides what's worth the time. Say what length the edit reached.

`timeline_transcript`, `timeline_propose` and `timeline_join_audit` cover what this needs.
