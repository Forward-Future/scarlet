# Dialogue pass

Make the conversation easier to follow with cuts alone. Keep the speaker’s meaning, the order of the exchange and the timing between synchronized tracks. The editor’s brief sets the pace and identifies any passage to protect. Use straight picture and audio cuts. Smoothing, mixing, color, framing, cutaways, music and captions each need an explicit request.

An abandoned start can come out when the next take completes the thought. A retake can begin just beyond the passage under review. Compare what each delivery actually says, particularly corrections, qualifications and changes of mind. Keep repetition used for emphasis and the other speaker’s reply. A direction to the recording crew can come out when the surrounding dialogue makes its purpose clear.

When a line is barely audible beside the dialogue on either side, usually cut the whole line and the wait around it. Keep a deliberate whisper or a quiet reply the audience needs to follow the exchange.

Cut tight. A pause earns its length by doing something: a beat before a reveal, a reaction, a breath between two ideas, time to take in what just appeared on screen. A pause that only waits comes down to the beat the delivery around it needs: next to nothing inside a sentence, a little more between sentences, more again where the subject changes. As a guide, not a rule, half a second or more with nothing happening in it plays as dead air. When nobody is speaking, keep only as long as the viewer needs to see the result: typing, loading, searching and waiting cut to the moment the result appears. A sigh, a cough, a lip smack or a throat-clear between lines goes out with the wait around it. So does a sound that isn’t the speaker’s, such as a system chime or a remark from someone off-mic, when nothing the audience needs is said over it. Cut an “um” or “uh” when the line flows better without it. A filler can also carry hesitation or hold a speaker’s turn. Use the words, measured sound and picture to decide. A gap in the transcript can contain speech or action.

Apply confident cuts with `dialogue_apply_cuts`. Mark a meaningful pause or protected phrase as a keep. When removing a passage could change the meaning or lose a reaction, stage the cut for the editor. Use `dialogue_review_inspect` and `dialogue_review_save` to record a recommendation that names the words or reaction at stake. Recommend keeping a cut only when its passage belongs in the video. When a cut that should go would clip a word at an edge, move the edge or ask the editor.

Check the joined dialogue in context. Each speaker’s words should play whole, with questions and answers still making sense together. A clipped word or an accidental restart that the cuts introduced or left behind is not finished work. Finish when the requested dialogue cuts and their review are ready.
