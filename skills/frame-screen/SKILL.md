# Frame the screen on what I'm doing

While the screen recording is on picture, the frame holds the part of the screen where the action is, and nothing else. The subject is the smallest region that holds what I'm doing or showing at that moment: a pane or panel, not the window around it, and a window, not the desktop around it.

- When I play, use or demo something, that pane fills the frame: the game, the preview, the canvas. Sidebars, chat panels, file lists, toolbars and the desktop around it stay out, even when they are part of the same window.
- When I read or talk about text, the passage fills the frame, large enough to read along, and the frame follows it down the page.
- When I point at one thing (a chart, a post, a result, a line of code), that thing fills the frame.

What I'm saying and where the action is on screen decide the subject. When it moves, for example from reading the agent's reply to playing what it built, the frame moves with it. It holds still through small pointer moves and only changes when the subject does.

## Zooming in and out

Every change of subject is a smooth zoom, never a jump. When I start referring to something, push in on it: the move starts just as I mention it and settles within about a second, easing in and out. When I move on, pull back out to the wider view, or go straight to the next subject if I name one right away, with the same easing. A zoom out is as smooth as a zoom in.

Hold each frame long enough to take in, at least a couple of seconds. If I touch on things faster than that, stay on a frame wide enough to hold them all instead of moving back and forth. The moves follow what I say, not the pointer.

Go tight by default. Widen only when the viewer needs to see two things together, such as a prompt and its result side by side. Check frames across the whole stretch, including mid-zoom, so the crop doesn't cut off a HUD, a control or a line I use a moment later.

My camera stays bottom right at the same size throughout. Place the subject so the camera doesn't cover the part being used or read.

`timeline_transcript`, `screen_text`, `frame_contact_sheet` and `timeline_propose` cover what this needs.
