# Matt’s talking-head edit

My style on top of the dialogue pass. A bare `/matt-talking-head` covers the current edit, including camera punch-ins and any screen demos. If I name a pass, source, selection or cut, work just on that. Keep an existing edit’s sync and composition. For separate camera, screen and mic recordings, play the dialogue mic once and carry cuts across all synchronized tracks, so my mouth, my voice and the action I describe still line up.

## Choose the take

Keep the delivery meant for the viewer. If the wording of two takes is the same but the splice changes which performance survives, keep the take I meant. Keep my genuine reactions.

Keep adjacent identical words when both belong in the sentence, as in “She checked in in Boston.” If the delivery makes it unclear whether one is a stumble, bring it to me for review with the full phrase and the word you would remove.

## Notes to my editor

I leave notes for my editor while I record. I often say them low, well under my speaking voice, right after the thing they are about: “You can get a screenshot of that. Here.” Others are at full voice: “Use the second take.” “Let me show you the second result” is for the viewer. Look for these notes through the whole recording, and treat a line said much quieter than the speech around it as a likely note.

Do what a note asks whenever Scarlet can: show the screenshot or the part of the screen I point at, put the two things I name side by side, use the take I ask for, cut what I say to cut. Say what you did in that edit’s summary and quote my words. A note you can’t carry out, such as an opening montage or a music cue, goes in your reply to me with my words and where I said them.

A note never plays in the finished video. Cut a clear one with its lead-in and the wait around it, and don’t flag it. If you can’t tell whether a line is a note or meant for the viewer, keep it and flag it with the doubt. Where a note runs into narration, cut the note alone and flag the join.

## My pace

I cut tight. At a join, leave one to two tenths of a second from the last word to the next, and three tenths at most between two ideas. A pause left inside kept footage comes down to about a third of a second between sentences. Six tenths or more with nothing happening in it is dead air. My published cuts run this tight. A reveal, a reaction or a change of subject still gets the time it earns.

Cut silent typing, searching and loading to the result, and hold the result long enough to see what changed. Flag any silence of two seconds or more that you keep in full, with what the viewer sees or hears during it.

Use a two-frame audio crossfade at dialogue joins and a straight picture cut. Bring my voice in promptly when the picture cuts back to me, especially in the first 40 seconds: about a tenth of a second from the picture cut to my first word on a straight pickup, closer to an eighth later on. Keep a longer beat for a demonstration, a reaction or a purposeful breath, and keep my timing where my voice already enters naturally. Protect the first consonant and the release of the outgoing word through the crossfade.

## Show the demo

During a synchronized screen demo, keep my camera inset at a consistent size in the bottom right. Start with the inset size you would otherwise use and scale it up by 20% in both dimensions, then crop the sides for a narrower frame. Aim for 1.5:1 (width to height), with room to go up to 2:1 if the framing needs it. Adjust the crop and zoom so my full chest stays in frame throughout the demo and my face has a little headroom. Round all four corners of the inset. Frame the moving recording around what I am showing. Fill the picture with the game or preview pane when I play it, the passage when I read it, or the chart and its labels when I discuss a result. Go wide enough to show both sides of a comparison or keep a tall demo intact. Keep controls I use and words I read clear of the inset.

Follow the narration. Ease into the subject as I mention it, settling in about a second. Hold through small pointer movements. Ease back out or across when the subject changes. When the narration moves quickly between related details, one wider frame can cover them. Check frames through the move and at the reveal for the label, control or result the viewer needs to see.

## Camera punch-ins

On full-screen camera footage, change the framing at every jump cut, wide to head and shoulders or back, so no cut jumps. Place them once the dialogue cuts are in, and again on any cut I approve later. Cuts close together can share the tight framing. Push in on a line that needs emphasis as well. About 115–130% is a starting point when the source stays sharp. Hold for a few seconds, follow my actual face position, and keep my eyes and mouth comfortably framed. Return wide at the end of the thought. Keep the demo inset steady.

## When requested

- **Captions.** Caption the retained speech in the project style. Correct misheard names, products and numbers. Break at spoken phrases, keeping names and units together, on one or two readable lines. Check them over the picture so the viewer can still see my face and the demo.
- **Slide B-roll.** Time each supplied slide or requested reveal to the words that introduce it. For new slide art, use one visual idea, a real screenshot, portrait, logo or headline where it helps, large type and few words. Keep the upper right open in the artwork. On a reveal, hold the setup in place and add only the new element. Put spoken detail and sourcing in the slide notes and check the rendered slide at video size.
