# Matt’s talking-head edit

My edit is a /dialogue-pass cut my way, with my screen demos framed and my camera punched in. Each part below calls the skill it builds on and says what I change. A bare `/matt-talking-head` covers the current edit, including camera punch-ins and any screen demos. If I name a pass, source, selection or cut, work just on that. Keep an existing edit’s sync and composition. For separate camera, screen and mic recordings, play the dialogue mic once and carry cuts across all synchronized tracks, so my mouth, my voice and the action I describe still line up.

When cropping my camera, make sure to show my full head and shoulders.

## Choose the take

Keep the delivery meant for the viewer. If the wording of two takes is the same but the splice changes which performance survives, keep the take I meant. Keep my genuine reactions.

Keep adjacent identical words when both belong in the sentence, as in “She checked in in Boston.” If the delivery makes it unclear whether one is a stumble, bring it to me for review with the full phrase and the word you would remove.

## Notes to my editor

I leave notes for my editor, Alex, while I record. I often say them low, well under my speaking voice, right after the thing they are about: “You can get a screenshot of that. Here.” Others are at full voice: “Use the second take.” “Let me show you the second result” is for the viewer. So is an ask to Alex that’s part of the show: said at full voice in the middle of what I’m telling the viewer, it tells them what they’re about to see, as in “Alex, put the other ones on the screen, please, so we can actually compare them.” Keep that line and do what it asks. How to do it is a note for Alex alone: in “Let me throw them side by side to you, Alex. Have your AI put side by side comparisons of the game here,” the first sentence plays and the rest is a note. Look for these notes through the whole recording, and treat a line said much quieter than the speech around it as a likely note.

Do what a note asks whenever Scarlet can: show the screenshot or the part of the screen I point at, put the two things I name side by side, use the take I ask for, cut what I say to cut. Say what you did in that edit’s summary and quote my words. A note you can’t carry out, such as an opening montage or a music cue, goes in your reply to me with my words and where I said them.

A note never plays in the finished video. Cut a clear one with its lead-in and the wait around it, and don’t flag it. If you can’t tell whether a line is a note or meant for the viewer, keep it and flag it with the doubt. Where a note runs into narration, cut the note alone and flag the join.

## My pace

I cut tight, and how tight depends on where the cut falls:

- A cut inside a sentence leaves next to no space. The next word follows my last as if I had said them in one breath, about five hundredths of a second from word to word.
- A cut at the end of a sentence leaves a very small space before the next one, about a tenth of a second from my last word to my next.
- Where no cut falls, leave my own pauses as I made them. A moment while I think, or a beat I hold for emphasis, is how I talk: up to about a second stays. A pause of about a second or more with nothing in it is dead air: it comes out, and its cut follows the two rules above.

Every cut is a jump on my camera, so cut where an editor would: at the mistakes and the dead air. Between them, let me play as I said it. The video should sound like me talking, never chopped up. Cuts that land one after another play as a stutter: a pause beside another cut goes out in that cut or stays, never in a cut of its own.

The two spacings above hold at every cut, one you place by its seconds included, and they start at my last word: whatever a cut keeps, my last word ends and the cut follows, with no beat of quiet between them. Once I finish a sentence, cut before I look away from the camera: that glance never plays. A reveal, a reaction or a change of subject still gets the time it earns, after the cut and before my voice, never after my last word.

Cut silent typing, searching and loading to the result, and hold the result long enough to see what changed. Flag any silence of two seconds or more that you keep in full, with what the viewer sees or hears during it.

Use a two-frame audio crossfade at dialogue joins and a straight picture cut. Bring my voice in the moment the picture cuts: about two frames from the cut to my voice, as my published cuts do, whether the line starts a sentence or carries one on, and right on the first sound of a word that starts softly. Hold the cut a few frames longer only where the line opens something new, such as a new topic, a demo or a reaction to what just appeared, or where a breath is part of how I say it: that beat plays after the cut, before my voice. A result on screen that needs a longer look gets it there too. Keep my timing where my voice already enters naturally. Protect the first consonant and the release of the outgoing word through the crossfade.

## Show the demo

/frame-screen, with these changes.

During a synchronized screen demo, my camera is an inset at one size throughout. Aim for the bottom right every time: first frame the screen so the inset there covers nothing I read or use, and move it to another corner only for a stretch where no such framing works. Start with the inset size you would otherwise use and scale it up by 20% in both dimensions, then crop the sides for a narrower frame. Aim for 1.5:1 (width to height), with room to go up to 2:1 if the framing needs it. Within the inset, frame me from a small margin above my hair down to the desk in front of me, with my full chest and my hands in and some of the room on either side of me, the same throughout the demo. Round all four corners of the inset.

Show the whole of what I am presenting with the rest of the page it is on: the whole watch on its web page, not a close-up of its movement. When the page is taller than the video, crop into it; cropping is fine. A move every ten seconds or so is fine while I go from one thing to the next.

## Camera punch-ins

/punch-in at every jump cut between sentences on full-screen camera footage. Keep the camera framing steady through mid-sentence cuts. Punch in or out mid-sentence only when my body position or placement in the frame changes enough to make the framing change necessary. That should be rare. Apply this rule once the dialogue cuts are in and again on any cut I approve later.

## When requested

- **Captions.** /add-captions in the project style.
- **Slide B-roll.** Time each supplied slide or requested reveal to the words that introduce it. For new slide art, use one visual idea, a real screenshot, portrait, logo or headline where it helps, large type and few words. Keep the upper right open in the artwork. On a reveal, hold the setup in place and add only the new element. Put spoken detail and sourcing in the slide notes and check the rendered slide at video size.
