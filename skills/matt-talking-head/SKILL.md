# Matt’s talking-head edit

This style is a /dialogue-pass cut tight, with screen demos framed and the camera punched in. Each part below calls the skill it builds on and says what it changes. A bare `/matt-talking-head` covers the current edit, including camera punch-ins and any screen demos. If I name a pass, source, selection or cut, work just on that. Keep an existing edit’s composition and sync. For separate camera, screen and mic recordings, play the dialogue mic once and carry cuts across all synchronized tracks so the mouth, voice and screen action stay lined up.

## Choose the take

Keep the delivery meant for the viewer. If the wording of two takes is the same but the splice changes which performance survives, keep the take I meant. Keep genuine reactions.

Keep adjacent identical words when both belong in the sentence, as in “She checked in in Boston.” If the delivery makes it unclear whether one is a stumble, bring it to me for review with the full phrase and the word you would remove.

## Notes to the editor

I leave notes for my editor while I record. I often say them low, well under my speaking voice, right after the thing they are about: “You can get a screenshot of that. Here.” Others are at full voice: “Use the second take.” “Let me show you the second result” is for the viewer. So is an on-camera ask that’s part of the show: said at full voice in the middle of what I’m telling the viewer, it tells them what they’re about to see, as in “Put the other ones on the screen, please, so we can actually compare them.” Keep that line and do what it asks. Instructions on how to do it are a note for the editor alone: in “Let me throw them side by side for you. Put side by side comparisons of the game here,” the first sentence plays and the rest is a note. Look for these notes through the whole recording, and treat a line said much quieter than the speech around it as a likely note.

Do what a note asks whenever Scarlet can: show the screenshot or the part of the screen I point at, put the two things I name side by side, use the take I ask for, cut what I say to cut. Say what you did in that edit’s summary and quote my words. A note you can’t carry out, such as an opening montage or a music cue, goes in your reply to me with my words and where I said them.

A note never plays in the finished video. Cut a clear one with its lead-in and the wait around it, and don’t flag it. If you can’t tell whether a line is a note or meant for the viewer, keep it and flag it with the doubt. Where a note runs into narration, cut the note alone and flag the join.

## Things to add later

Sometimes I introduce something for the viewer to see or hear — like “let me show you…”, “take a look at this”, “watch this” or “here’s what he said” — when it clearly isn’t on my screen recording and isn’t in the project’s Media. Keep my line, don’t make or look up a replacement on your own, and leave a To do at that spot saying what needs to be added and quoting my line, plus list it in your reply with where it lands. If I also left a note to the editor for the same insert, cut the note and leave one To do for the spot.

## Pace

I cut tight, and how tight depends on where the cut falls:

- A cut inside a sentence leaves next to no space. The next word follows my last as if I had said them in one breath, about five hundredths of a second from word to word.
- A cut at the end of a sentence leaves a very small space before the next one, about a tenth of a second from my last word to my next.
- Where no cut falls, leave my own pauses as I made them. A moment while I think, or a beat I hold for emphasis, is how I talk: up to about a second stays. A pause of about a second or more with nothing in it is dead air: it comes out, and its cut follows the two rules above.

Every cut is a jump on camera, so cut where an editor would: at the mistakes and the dead air. Between them, let me play as I said it. The video should sound like me talking, never chopped up. Cuts that land one after another play as a stutter: a pause beside another cut goes out in that cut or stays, never in a cut of its own. A reaction or line under about a second left between two cuts plays as a stutter too: keep one of the pauses beside it so it plays on with the speech around it, or cut it out with the waits either side.

The two spacings above hold at every cut, one you place by its seconds included, on both sides of it: my last word ends and the cut follows, and after the cut my voice comes straight in. Once I finish a sentence, cut before I look away from the camera: that glance never plays. A change of subject, a new demo or a reaction gets no quiet of its own at a cut on camera: the jump already marks it, and quiet on my face before or after the cut plays as empty space. A reveal or a result gets the time it earns on the screen, where there is something to look at.

Cut silent typing, searching and loading to the result, and hold the result long enough to see what changed. Flag any silence of two seconds or more that you keep in full, with what the viewer sees or hears during it.

Use a two-frame audio crossfade at dialogue joins and a straight picture cut. Bring my voice in the moment the picture cuts: about two frames from the cut to my voice, whether the line starts a sentence, carries one on or opens a new topic, and right on the first sound of a word that starts softly. Where the screen plays across the cut and shows a result that needs a longer look, hold it before my voice; on camera, keep only a breath that is part of how I say the line. Keep my timing where my voice already enters naturally. Protect the first consonant and the release of the outgoing word through the crossfade.

## Show the demo

/frame-screen, with these changes.

During a synchronized screen demo, keep the camera inset at one steady size and corner throughout, and frame the screen around it so what I talk about, read or use stays clear of the inset. Only move the camera inset for a stretch if no framing keeps the subject clear, and say why in that edit’s reason.

Show the whole of what I am presenting with the rest of the page it is on. When framing my screen, keep the browser's own interface (tabs, address bar, bookmarks bar) out of frame wherever the framing allows, and show only the page content. When the page is taller than the video, crop into it; cropping is fine. A move every ten seconds or so is fine while I go from one thing to the next.

## Camera punch-ins

/punch-in at every jump cut between sentences on full-screen camera footage. Keep the camera framing steady through mid-sentence cuts. Punch in or out mid-sentence only when my body position or placement in the frame changes enough to make the framing change necessary. That should be rare. Apply this rule once the dialogue cuts are in and again on any cut I approve later.

## When requested

- **Captions.** /add-captions in the project style.
- **Slide B-roll.** Time each supplied slide or requested reveal to the words that introduce it. For new slide art, use one visual idea, a real screenshot, portrait, logo or headline where it helps, large type and few words. Keep the upper right open in the artwork. On a reveal, hold the setup in place and add only the new element. Put spoken detail and sourcing in the slide notes and check the rendered slide at video size.
